## Context

Nordpay is a Copenhagen-based payments app. Growth was healthy at the top of the
funnel — around 30,000 sign-up starts a month — but only 43% of people who began
onboarding ever made their first transaction. Marketing was paying for users the
product then lost. I was brought in as the only designer on a squad of five to
own the onboarding flow end to end.

## The problem, precisely

"Improve onboarding" is not a brief. The first two weeks went into narrowing it:

- Amplitude funnels showed two dominant drop points: the ID verification step
  (−31%) and the "link your bank" step (−18%).
- Support tickets told us *why* for one of them: people didn't have their
  passport at hand and the flow offered no way back in. Abandoning meant
  starting over.
- Twelve user interviews added the second reason: the bank-linking screen asked
  for credentials before explaining what Nordpay would do with them. Several
  participants used the word "phishing."

<PullQuote attribution="Interview participant, week 2">
  This is the screen where I'd text my brother and ask if this app is legit.
</PullQuote>

So the reframed problem: **the flow demanded trust and documents before it had
earned or explained either.**

## Process

I mapped the existing 14-screen flow against the two questions users were
actually asking — "what do I get?" and "what do you take?" — and found the flow
answered neither before screen 9.

Three design bets came out of that mapping, each cheap to test separately:

1. **Reorder, don't redesign.** Let people into a read-only version of the app
   before KYC, moving verification to the moment they first add money — when
   motivation is highest.
2. **Make leaving safe.** Save progress server-side and send a resume link, so
   "I'll do this when I find my passport" stops meaning "never."
3. **Explain before asking.** A single plain-language screen before bank
   linking: what we access, what we never see, who regulates us.

I tested all three as separate Maze prototypes with 8 users each rather than
one big redesign — when a combined prototype wins, you don't know which change
did the work.

## Decisions and reasoning

**The hard argument was bet #1.** Compliance initially read "KYC later" as "KYC
optional," which it isn't. We spent a week with the legal team mapping exactly
which actions require verified identity under Danish AML rules, and found
browsing rates and seeing the fee calculator required none. The final flow
verifies identity at first deposit — later, but not optional. This is the kind
of constraint negotiation I'd rather show than hide: the design only worked
because the compliance conversation happened.

**I killed my favorite idea.** An animated progress companion tested well for
delight but added 11 seconds median to the flow and two participants asked if
it was "a kids' app." Charm lost to speed, correctly.

## Outcome

Eight weeks after launch, measured on the same Amplitude funnel:

- Completed onboarding: **43% → 61%** (the headline 40% relative lift)
- Resume-link emails recovered **9%** of abandoners within 72 hours
- "Is this safe/legit" support tickets down **56%**
- Time-to-first-transaction unchanged — we moved verification later without
  slowing activation

What I'd do differently: start the compliance conversation in week 1, not week
5. The legal constraints turned out to be the real design material, and I lost
a month treating them as a review step instead of an input.
